So I have a question for you. Has someone ever asked you a question that you know the answer to, but you didn't want to give it? And so, like the guys in the story today, you responded saying, “I don't know.” Did you ever do that before?

There are some people that should say “I don't know” more often. We as parents can be guilty of that sometimes. Our kids ask us questions that we feel unprepared to answer. But because we feel like we have to know everything, we give them the best answer we can think of, whether or not we're sure it's right.

The other day, we were having dinner and Sion asked me, “How does electricity work?” And so I thought, “I got this.” I started drawing pictures. I started using the words that I thought I remembered from science class. But the more he kept asking me about it, the more I realized I had no idea what I was talking about. And I should have just said, “I don't know.” I think a lot of problems would be resolved with an honest “I don't know.”

But the story that we read about today is the opposite of that. It's where people know the truth, but they say, “I don't know” instead. Now, let me tell you one time when I'm pretty confident that all of you said, “I don't know” when you actually knew the answer to the question. You're on your way home from school, and your parents ask you, “What did you do today? What happened today?” And what do you say? “I don't know,” or maybe you might say, “Nothing.”

But is that really the answer to the question? You go to school, and a thousand things happen that your parents want to know about. But when they ask you, you say, “Nothing. I don't know.”

Sometimes, if there's a test and you don't do well, you might tell your parents nothing much happened at school. We've all been there before. Why do you think it is that when we know that things happen to us in school that are important, and our parents ask us about it, we don't tell them?

You don't want to deal with it. You don't want to talk about it. They might ask you more questions. Sometimes we think that we don't want to talk about certain things because if we answer our parents' questions, they're going to ask more questions, right? They're going to want to know more, and maybe we might have to say things that we don't want to say. Or they might tell us to do something that we don't want to do. So instead of being open and honest, we say, “I don't know,” because that's easy.

Today, Jesus was asking people a question. It was an important question, and it was a question that they knew the answer to. But instead of giving an honest answer, they said, “We don't know.” Do you think they tricked Jesus? No. Jesus knew what was in their hearts. They knew what the right answer to his question was, but they didn't want to get caught saying something that they knew would get them in trouble.

He asks them a question about John the Baptist. John the Baptist is Jesus' cousin, and he was baptizing people. That's why he's John the Baptist. And the way that he was telling people about God was upsetting these people. They didn't agree with what he was saying. But they knew that, in fact, what he was doing was good and from God.

If they answered honestly, then Jesus would have said, “Why are you guys giving this guy a hard time if you know that what he's doing is from God?” But they didn't want to do that because they didn't want to get caught lying or being hypocritical. So instead, they just said, “I don't know.”

Shouldn't they be honest? They absolutely should be honest. And if they were honest, if they said the truth, then it would have been good for them. Because then they would have accepted the thing they knew in their hearts, which is that they should listen to John the Baptist and they should listen to Jesus. But because they didn't want to accept the truth, they lied and said, “I don't know.”

Now, the question that we have for us today is, are we going to be like them? Are we going to say that we don't know the truth when we really do, when it really matters? Because we're not supposed to try to be like them. We're supposed to be like Jesus wants us to be, and to be honest and truthful, because in the end, that helps us. That makes us better people.

Here's what I want you guys to do when you go home from church today. I want you to tell your parents about something that happened to you this week that they don't know about. There's always things that happen at school that they don't know about. But tell them something honestly. It'll make you feel good and it'll make them feel good too.

And most importantly, when you're in church, you have to be honest with Jesus about the things that we've done that we shouldn't have done when we confess. That's why you guys participated in confession today. When we open our hearts to Jesus, then the truth will set us free.
