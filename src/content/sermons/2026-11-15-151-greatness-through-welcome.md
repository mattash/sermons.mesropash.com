Over these past weeks, we have been speaking about what it means to be a Dndes, someone entrusted with the care of a household. Today we take that step together at St. John. We are taking up Dndes as the way we understand and live our parish membership: accepting, together, the care of God’s household.

We began at the foot of the Cross, where Christ gave Himself for us before we could offer anything to Him. We heard His call to be a family that hears His Word and does it. And last week, after Jesus raised Jairus’s daughter, He told those around her to give her something to eat. He gave life, and entrusted her ordinary, continuing care to them. Now we come to another question: What kind of stewards is Christ asking us to become?

In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells His disciples that He will be handed over to men. He is speaking about the suffering ahead. Luke tells us that they do not understand; the meaning is hidden from them, and they are afraid to ask. Then an argument arises about which of them is the greatest.

Jesus takes a child and places the child beside Him. Listen to what He says: “Whoever receives this child in my name receives me.” And He goes on to say that whoever receives Him receives the One who sent Him.

Stay with that for a moment. They are trying to work out who is greatest, and Jesus directs their attention to this child. The person they might have overlooked becomes the person through whom they are asked to receive their Lord. That tells us something about being a Dndes. The household entrusted to us is full of people whom Christ asks us to receive in His name.

Now, we all need to know that we matter. We can serve for years and wonder whether anyone notices. We can come to church and still wonder whether there is a place for us. Those needs deserve care. The difficulty begins when I can only feel important if someone else is less important—when another person’s welcome feels like the loss of my own place.

Jesus tells the disciples that the one who is least among them is great. He calls us to give up the contest for first place. Our worth is held by God, and we can give another person our attention without having to prove our own importance. Jesus Himself is going toward the Cross, where He will give Himself in love. He is teaching the disciples to follow Him on that road.

Then John tells Jesus about a man casting out demons in His name. They tried to stop him because he was not following with their group. Jesus tells them to let him continue. The disciples do not own His name. Our life in the Church should teach us to recognize Christ’s grace at work in another person and to rejoice in it. A Dndes is entrusted with God’s household. It remains God’s household.

Think of someone walking through the doors of our church, unsure where to stand. We may be busy, already in a conversation, already on our way to do something. We can go over, stand beside them, and help them find their place in the service. That is one way we begin caring for the household. As we heard last week, care continues: we learn their name, greet them again, and take time to know them.

Their place among us matters before we know what they can contribute. And as their life becomes part of our life, we may need to make room for a gift or an idea we had not expected. We may need to share work we have always done ourselves. Being entrusted with care asks us to welcome others into that care too.

This is where these four weeks have been leading us. Today I am inviting you to become a Dndes: to take up a lasting commitment to the worship, the people, and the life of St. John. When we say, “This is my church,” we are also saying, “These are people entrusted to my care, and I am entrusted to theirs.”

Through Baptism and Chrismation, Christ has already welcomed us into His life. He feeds us at His Table. We are responding to a belonging we have received by grace. A financial pledge does not purchase our place in Christ’s Church. If you have never pledged, you are still welcome here, and this invitation is for you too.

That shared care takes four forms: Worship, Serve, Participate, and Pledge.

We worship: we gather to pray, hear His Word, and receive the life Christ gives us in the Divine Liturgy. We serve: we offer our time and gifts in the care of others. We participate: we share in the fellowship, learning, and responsibilities of parish life. And we pledge: we commit our financial support thoughtfully and regularly, according to our means, so that this care can continue.

What we can offer will differ with our health, our families, and the season of life we are in. There are also times when we need to receive care. We share responsibility for the same household, with different gifts and different limits.

Many of you have carried this responsibility faithfully for years. Thank you. If you are already a pledged member, your commitment continues. There is no need to apply again; your annual pledge and your existing rights and responsibilities continue. I invite you to renew your intention before God and choose one of these four practices in which to grow.

If you have never been “a pledged member”, and especially if you never heard of such a thing or understood what it was about, I invite you to take this new step with our parish: become a Dndes. This week, visit the Dndes page on our parish website, consider these four commitments prayerfully, and use the membership application there to take that step. If you have questions, or would like help finding your part in this care, speak with me or the parish office.

In this Badarak, Christ gives Himself to us. We receive His Body and Blood, and we receive His life together. When we leave the sanctuary, the people beside us are still our brothers and sisters. Let us accept the care He entrusts to us, and carry it faithfully together. We began at the foot of the Cross. May all our care for this household grow from the love of the One who first gave Himself for us, our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ.

Amen.
