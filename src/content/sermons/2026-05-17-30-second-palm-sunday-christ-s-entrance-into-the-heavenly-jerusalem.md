[English sermon]
In the lectionary, in the calendar of the Armenian Church, a lot of us we like to note things that are unique to the Armenian tradition. There are so many things that we share with our sister churches, of course, a creed of faith and the core holidays that serve as the foundation for our Christian life with our sister churches and with the broader family of Christianity in the world. But there are some things that are truly unique to the Armenian Church that you just won't find anyplace else. The day that we celebrate today in Armenian tradition is one of those things. It's called the Second Palm Sunday. Second Palm Sunday is the Sunday that follows the feast of the Ascension of Jesus Christ.

The Bible tells us very clearly that Christ had gathered his disciples at the Mount of Ascension, where the place had been appointed, and from there Christ ascended to heaven, leaving with them a great commission. Առաքելութիւն տվավ աշակերտներուն, այդ պահին և ատկե ըս կըսավ. «Եկեղեցու առաքելութիւնը»։ He says, "Therefore go and baptize all nations in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, teaching them to obey everything that I have commanded you." And so this happens. Christ ascends. Now, this was what happened in the church on Thursday. Today is Sunday. Today is the second Palm Sunday.

Where does this tradition come from, and why is it unique to our church? The story comes to us from the historians of our tradition that Saint Gregory the Illuminator was imprisoned in the tomb, which we call Khor Virap, the deep pit. During his long imprisonment, he was visited by an angel who comforted him and sustained him throughout his tribulation and isolation. And it was on the Sunday following Ascension that the angel didn't come. And the following day, Monday, Saint Gregory was curious: "You have visited me every day. Why didn't you come yesterday?" And the angel responds to Saint Gregory, "Because all of us were celebrating the return of our King, Jesus Christ." This time he entered the heavenly Jerusalem on the Sunday following the Ascension, when he ascended to heaven. So Christ ascends to heaven, and then the angels, they welcome him back into the heavenly kingdom.

Now, this is a revelation that comes through divine means by an angel to a saint of our church, Saint Gregory the Illuminator. And on the basis of this testimony, our church adopted the practice of recognizing the Sunday following the Ascension as the feast or the celebration of the Second Palm Sunday of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. But it isn't isolated in its concept and its importance to the testimony of Saint Gregory the Illuminator. We find in here an important witnessing about the nature of the recognition of Jesus Christ's power and authority in the world.

I want to read to you from the Epistle to the Hebrews in chapter nine. It says here, beginning from verse twenty-four, which in this Bible, in a section is titled "Christ's Sacrifice Takes Away Sin." "Thus it was necessary for the sketches of the heavenly things to be purified with these rites, but the heavenly things themselves need better sacrifices than these. For Christ did not enter a sanctuary made by human hands, a mere copy of the true one, but he entered into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf. Nor was it to offer himself again and again, as the high priest enters the holy place year after year with the blood that is not his own. But then he would have to suffer again and again since the foundation of the world. But as it is, he has appeared once and for all at the end of the ages and removed sin by the sacrifice of himself."

Here the epistle is testifying to the truth of something that was very interesting. Christ entered into the earthly Jerusalem, what he's referring to as a copy. The earthly Jerusalem, the earthly temple is an imperfect facsimile of the heavenly and glorious reality that is the heavenly Jerusalem. As Christ entered into Jerusalem during the first Palm Sunday, he did it as what we call in our Orthodox faith a prefiguration. This is something that's happening in advance so that we'll recognize the true thing when it eventually happens. When Christ entered into the imperfect earthly Jerusalem, he found himself surrounded by people giving thanks and celebrating for his coming, who did not understand him and who did not truly love him. They received him, but as the gospel reading that we read just at the conclusion of the Badarak said, "They did not understand him. They apprehended him not." This was the imperfect Palm Sunday that led to Christ's crucifixion.

But that which was imperfect is perfected through Christ, and we witness this on the second Palm Sunday. Instead of entering the earthly Jerusalem, Christ enters the heavenly Jerusalem, and there it is perfected, his glory is coming. He is received into the heavenly abode by those who truly honor and glorify him, by those who receive him for the truth of what he is and what he has come to do.
