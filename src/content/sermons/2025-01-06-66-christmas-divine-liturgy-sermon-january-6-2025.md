Good morning, beloved faithful. Christ is born and revealed. Blessed is the revelation of Jesus Christ, and this is good news for you and I. Welcome to you all here this beautiful Armenian Christmas morning as we prepare ourselves to fully celebrate this blessed occasion. Of course, we find ourselves deep into the Christmas season, having been celebrating for quite some time, but yet here gathered together we are honoring one of the most important traditions associated with the Armenian Apostolic Church. A thousand years ago, a Byzantine prince came to Saint Nerses Shnorhali, whose icon is on the right side of the church, and he challenged Saint Nerses Shnorhali, and he said, "Why is your church different, and why should I honor that?" He belonged to the Byzantine Church, or we would call it the Eastern Orthodox Church today. And with sincerity, he asked him, he asked Saint Nerses, "Can you explain to me how your church is different from mine, and why should I be okay with that?" And of course, because Saint Nerses Shnorhali was so graceful and grace-filled in his words, he was able to encapsulate in a beautiful letter all of the ways and reasons why, stemming from the fifth century and earlier than that even, the Armenian Church has developed a very unique and quintessential expression of Christianity that resonates with and is a reflection of the Armenian spirit. And one of the things that he emphasizes in this letter is the Armenian tradition of celebrating the Theophany and Nativity of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ, on January 6th. And being so gracious as he is, he did not proclaim that this was the one true date of Christmas that only the true faithful honor. But he said, indeed, it is a grace-filled day, that it is a day that belongs to the Lord, that the Armenian people will never abandon as the day that we celebrate the birth of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. Of course, there are other traditions, but in the end, we all honor and give thanks to the same God and the gift of the incarnation of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. Today, with your presence in this sanctuary, you are honoring that same commitment that was expressed by Saint Nerses the Grace-filled one thousand years ago. And of course, the forebearers of our church so wisely sought to honor this, the most traditional date associated with the birth of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ.

I spoke to you earlier about the orthodox nature of our celebration of Christmas and how it was explained so beautifully by Saint Nerses the Grace-filled. And there's another aspect of the Orthodox celebration of Christmas which we're about to be reminded of in just a moment, and that is its unbreakable and inextricable connection with the revelation of Jesus Christ. And when we speak about revelation, it happens throughout the history of mankind. Revelation is a process and not a moment. Revelation begins from the creation of the cosmos. And when we read the Old Testament, we see that constantly there are bits and pieces of revelation of the coming of Jesus Christ happening over and over and over again throughout the history of humanity, all of these things building up to the climax that is the incarnation of Jesus Christ and everything that was to be foretold. All of these revelations are things for us. They're all little gifts that have happened throughout history so that we can know and understand God better, despite our human frailties and our imperfections and our inability to comprehend the infinite and incomprehensible nature of the creator God, who is greater than all things.

Recently, I've often been struggling for my own part to explain to my son, Sion, some of these really difficult to understand concepts. Lately, Sion has been asking me which planet we have to go to to get to heaven. He's been studying space. He's such a scientific mind. And it's a good question. It's not in the next town. It's not in a different country. It's not under the water. It's not in the sky. We've made all that very clear. If it's in none of these places, then it's got to be in outer space someplace. And we tell him it's not in outer space. He says, "Baba, what's left? Where else can it be?" And I said, "It's outside of all of these things. It's something else." And of course, it's blowing his mind. It's blowing my mind. But we know this because God has revealed it to us. We don't have to guess. This is what he told us the truth was about himself throughout the history of mankind, this much and so much more.

And the epiphanal moment, the climax moment of revelation happens during Christ's baptism. That is the fulfillment of revelation, the perfection of revelation, when everything comes together. Christ is fully revealed to us as a member of the Holy Trinity in that moment of his baptism. And so the incarnation is that inextricable moment that leads us to this baptism that tells us fully and completely: ah, this is it. This is the Holy Trinity. This is the thing. This is the triune Godhead. This is God. Now it's all in front of us, and we've got what we need in order to find our path to heaven. And how do we begin that path? We begin it with baptism.

I was sharing in my Armenian message that I recently went to the Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin to participate in these training programs for all of the priests of the Armenian church throughout the world.
