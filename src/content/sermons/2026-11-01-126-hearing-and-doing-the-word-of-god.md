Last week, on the Sunday of our food festival, I began this sermon series talking more about what it means for us to belong and be deeply engaged in the life of a parish, and in particular, this parish, St. John. I concluded that sermon with a question for the community: How do you care for St. John, and how might you care for it more faithfully?

For those of you who may have been reflecting on that topic, perhaps you thought of somebody you might want to reach out to, a person in the community that you can connect with more deeply. Maybe you thought about asking, “Is there anything that I can help with?” Maybe you're still thinking about it. I hope you are. I know that I still am, despite the fact that I've been trying to be someone who cares for this parish for the past fourteen years. It's all right if we don't have it all figured out right now. I want us to keep that question with us, especially this morning, because today's Gospel helps us to understand what we are invited to do in order to answer it.

When we spoke last week, we talked about gathering underneath the Holy Cross of Jesus Christ. It was the Feast of the Discovery of the Holy Cross, and that was important. He brings us to himself and to one another in front of his cross. He gathers us and teaches us how to live as a church, as a community, as a family. How so?

In today's Gospel, Jesus is surrounded by a crowd. Not for the first time do we hear about the crowd being so thick that people have trouble getting access to him. His mother and his family have come to see him, but they cannot reach him. Physically, they are barred from approaching him. Someone comes to Christ and tells him that his mother and brothers are standing outside waiting to see him. And Jesus answers, “My mother and my brothers are those who hear the Word of God and do it.”

Hear the Word of God and do it. It's such a very simple lesson, but we can and should spend the rest of our lives learning how to live it. We know that hearing someone and responding to them are two very different things. In our relationships with brothers and sisters, husbands and wives, parents and children, we all know what it means to hear somebody physically. It is often the next part that's the hardest: to do something about what we hear. We can understand every word that somebody is saying, and we can even agree that it's important. But most of us go on with our day and nothing quite changes.

The same thing can happen when we hear the Gospel. We hear Christ speak about forgiveness. We hear him and we know that it's right, but there is still somebody in our lives that we simply cannot forgive. We hear him speak about loving our neighbors, but we hurry past the person who needs our attention the most when it's inconvenient. We hear that we have been entrusted with the care of one another, and we think, “I'm sure somebody else will take care of it.”

I say “we” because this is a question for me too. I'm preaching the Gospel today, but that does not mean that I have finished learning how to respond to God's Word, by any means. Just before Jesus speaks about his family, he tells the people to pay attention to how they listen. So let us ask ourselves today: What happens to the Word that we hear when we leave this sanctuary?

Now, there's something important for us to remember. Last Sunday we said that our life in the church begins with a gift of Jesus Christ. Father Ghadabed spoke about this. I love what he said as we were waiting for the children to gather for the confession today: that it is a gift and not something that we can earn. We spoke about that last week, and it's very important for us always to remember it. Everything that we offer in this church grows out of what has already been given to us, not what we hope to receive because of what we say or do.

In today's Epistle, Saint Paul speaks about that gift. He tells the Ephesians that God has adopted us through Jesus Christ. He speaks about forgiveness, the forgiveness that we receive in Christ, and the Holy Spirit with whom we are sealed through baptism. He blesses God for the abundance of this grace. But think about that word: adopted. We become inheritors. God has welcomed us as his very children, as his most dear children, and he has brought us into relationship by his grace with him and with one another. We don't have to earn that invitation by proving how useful we can be.

As we said last week, through baptism and chrismation we've already been made a part of God's family, this family that we are with today. And now, by his grace, we're learning how to live the life that we have freely received. There's real effort to be made there. There is work that has to be done. Forgiveness can be difficult, to say the least. Giving somebody our attention when it costs our time is not easy. Caring for our parish asks and demands something of us. But we are doing these things as people who are loved by God and who are learning to share that love with others.

When Jesus speaks about his mother in this Gospel, we should also remember what Saint Luke has already told us about her. Long before this crowd had gathered before Jesus Christ, Mary heard the Word of God. The angel Gabriel came to her with a message that would change her whole life. And the Holy Mother of God answered, “Let it be according to your word,” when it was revealed to her that she would be the bearer of God. She heard and she answered with her whole life.

The Holy Mother of God shows us what it means to receive God's Word faithfully and to put it into practice in our lives. She trusted him without knowing every detail of what would lie ahead of her. Yet her whole life became a life of love, faithfulness, and sacrifice. We honor the Holy Mother of God, Saint Mary, in our prayers and in our hymns, and we ask for her intercession.

Here is something that we can learn from her this morning: When God calls us, we can begin with a faithful answer to what has been placed before us. We may not know everything that follows. We cannot know everything that will follow. We may not know how much we can do or what we can offer. But what we can offer is our yes.

This brings us back to the word dundas. A steward is someone entrusted with the care of the household. At Saint John, we're inviting one another to put that belonging into practice through the care of this parish and its people. That begins with listening to Christ, and as we listen to him, we learn to pay attention to one another. Who's carrying a burden today? I know that we all came here carrying something heavy. Who's come here and does not yet know anyone in this parish? I'm sure there are a few of you. I know there are a few of you here today.

Perhaps somebody has already come to mind, and perhaps you know a need and you have something to offer. You could call somebody that you haven't seen in a while. You could introduce yourself to somebody after badarak, learn their name, and learn more about them. You could offer a particular skill or a little bit of your time for this parish and its family, for a need that you know about, or maybe inquire if there is one you do not know about. Perhaps the most faithful thing that you can do this week is to begin repairing a relationship in your life that has become strained, for all of these things are God-pleasing.

Some of you are already giving everything that you have to get through a difficult time in your lives. I know what that's like. Your response could be a prayer, a short conversation, or allowing someone else to help you. We're learning to care for one another here. There will be times when we offer that care, and there are times when we are the ones who need to receive it. It's important that we play both of those roles.

So let us return to the small answer that came to mind last week. And if you're still looking for one, listen to Christ's word again today. What word of Christ will you be putting into practice this week? Let's give that answer a little bit at a time, a little attention, and a place in our actual day-to-day lives. Let's carry what we've heard here in this sanctuary into the hall, into our homes, and into the encounters that await us in the week to come.

Christ has gathered us to himself. He's welcomed us into his family and made us family with one another. May he give us the grace to hear this word and to do it, caring faithfully for one another for the glory of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Amen.

Now, let's review what we just talked about in fancy words with our kids. What is it that you heard from the message that I shared with you? Is there anything that you heard in all those big words? Wasn't it interesting when Jesus said about his mom and his brothers that those who hear what he says and do it are his family?

Now, do you think his mom heard what he said and did it? You're right. His mom was definitely part of his family, because she was the one who heard him the clearest and always did what he said. Certainly, his brothers, his disciples, listened to him, and they did their best. But we sometimes have a hard time listening and doing, right? Especially at home when it's time to fold the laundry. Especially when there's something fun that you want to do, and the thing that you have to do, that you're being told to do, doesn't seem like it's fun. That can be really hard.

What are some tricks that can help us do things that seem hard? What do you do when it's time to do your homework but you don't want to? You listen to music. That's a great life hack. When it's time to clean your room or do your homework and you don't want to, put on some music you want to listen to. You can make the thing that's hard for you to do fun by listening to music that you enjoy.

Isn't it interesting that when we come to church, the first thing that we hear when we walk in through the door is music? It makes it feel different, right? It touches us in a different way. And this is love from God trying to say, “I love you. Come to this place so we can have fun together. I know this seems hard, but it doesn't have to be.”

For you guys, I want you to remember that a life with God means being invited to be a part of his family, which you all were through your baptism, and listening to him when he's talking to us through the church, through the Bible, and then doing our best to do the things that he teaches us, even when it's hard. Then we get to be family with one another, and truly, that can be one of the things that makes us feel the most happy and peaceful in our lives. God bless you guys.
