Հոր և Որդվո և Հոգվույն Սրբո, ամեն։

Այս ամիսը՝ հոկտեմբեր ամիսը, մեր ժողովրդի կյանքում կնշվի որպես հայկական մշակույթի ամիսը, որի ընթացքին մենք կհիշենք, կաջակցենք և իրարու կհիշեցնենք մեր մշակույթի ժառանգությունը։ Եվ ասիկա ի՞նչ առիթով հիմնավորված է։ Առիթն է, որ Թարգմանչաց տոնը կտոնախմբվի այս ամսվա մեջ, և նաև այլ մի շարք տոներ, որոնք շատ կարևոր են։ Հայերս արդեն շատ իրազեկ ենք և ծանոթ կգտնենք Թարգմանչաց տոնը, որի ընթացքին կհիշենք Սուրբ Մեսրոպը, Դավիթ Փիլիսոփան, Սուրբ Գրիգոր Նարեկացի, Սուրբ Ներսես Շնորհալի և Մովսես Խորենացի և այլն։

Եվ այս առիթով, բնականաբար, մենք պետք է հիշենք այդ մեծ ժառանգությունը, որ մինչև այսօր կվայելենք մեր առօրյա կյանքում։ Եկեղեցին այսօր գոյություն ունի այդ Թարգմանչաց տոնի սրբերու շնորհիվ, և շարունակություն կա եկեղեցվո կյանքում։ Բայց կուզեմ ձեզի հիշեցնել, որ ուրիշ տոներ կան այս ամսվա մեջ, որոնք շատ կարևոր են, օրինակի համար՝ եկեղեցվո վարդապետներու տոնը։ Այդ օրը մենք կհիշենք ոչ հայկական, բայց կարևոր եկեղեցվո հայրեր, որոնք անհրաժեշտ էին, էական էին մեր եկեղեցվո ձևավորման համար, և իրենց աստվածաբանական ներդրումով կարողացան հաստատել եկեղեցվո աստվածաբանությունը մինչև այսօր, օրինակի համար՝ Ոսկեբերանը և այլն։ Ուրեմն այս անունները մեզի համար մինչև այսօր կարևոր են, որովհետև առանց իրենց մենք չպիտի ըլլայինք։

Եվ երեկ ուրիշ կարևոր տոն կար, և այդ տոնը կկոչվեր Ավետարանիչների տոնը՝ Մատթեոս, Մարկոս, Ղուկաս և Հովհաննես։ Եվ այդ առիթով պետք է հիշենք, որ այս եկեղեցին իր անվանակոչության օրը կհիշի, որովհետև ասիկա Սուրբ Հովհաննես Ավետարանիչի եկեղեցին է։ Եվ այդ օրը մենք պետք է հիշենք, թե դա ինչ կնշանակե մեզի համար, որովհետև այս բոլոր անձերը, ներառյալ ավետարանիչները, բոլորը իրենց հերթին թարգմանիչներ եղած են մեր հավատքի, ովքեր թարգմանած են մեր հավատքը մեկ լեզուեն մյուս լեզուն՝ հույներեն, հայերեն, ասորեն և այլն։ Իրենք ապահոված են հասկացողությունը և գիտակցությունը մեր ժողովրդի համար։

Բայց թարգմանությունը ոչ միայն կպատահի լեզվով, այլ նաև միտքով, զգացողությամբ, մշակույթով և այլն, որովհետև այդ ամեն ինչը մի ձևով պետք է մեր մտքին և սրտին հասնի։ Եվ մինչև այսօր պետք է գնահատենք ատիկա, որովհետև պետք է այդ լուրը, այդ Ավետիսը մեր սրտի մեջ ընկնի, որ սերունդներով թարգմանված էր։ Ում համա՞ր։ Մեզի համար, ինձի համար, ձեզի համար, որ ըմբռնելի ըլլա, որ հասկանալի ըլլա, որ հասանելի և մատչելի ըլլա։ Ուրեմն հարգենք այդ ներդրումը, հարգենք այդ զոհաբերությունը, որովհետև մինչև այսօր իրենց գործը պտղաբեր է և կաշխատի ի փառս ամենասուրբ Երրորդության։

Եվ ուրիշ խոսքով կուզեմ ըսել, որ մենք ալ կրնանք թարգմանիչներ ըլլալ։ Թարգմանիչ ենք, երբ որ մենք կբերենք Քրիստոսի սերը ուրիշներու կյանքերուն, որովհետև այդ սերը, որ հասավ մեր սիրտը, մեր գործերով, ոչ միայն խոսքով, բայց նաև մտքով և գործով կփոխանցվի ուրիշներուն մեր շուրջը։ Ուրեմն, ես կուզեմ մանավանդ հաջորդ շաբաթ, որ շատ հյուրեր պիտի ունենանք, այս հյուրերուն մեր հյուրասիրությամբ, մեր բարի վարելակերպով, մեր օրինակով, մեր սիրով պետք է ընդունինք և թարգմանենք մեր հավատքը իրենց համար, որ իրենք տեսնեն Քրիստոսը մեր մեջ է։

I was not here last Sunday because I had the blessing to participate in the ACYO fall retreat. It is, for me, a treasured experience, and I have the opportunity to connect with young people in their lives and the challenges that they're facing on a day-to-day basis.

I'm going to tell you the story of a young man. It's not the story of a specific person, but it very accurately describes the reality that many of our young people are facing. By young people, in particular, I'm talking about those who are in college or post-college, living on their own and establishing their lives.

He grew up in a household that was culturally Armenian and faithful by nature, though maybe not necessarily as devout as others, but certainly had a connection to the church and the community. He had in his heart already a firmly established identity as an Armenian Christian. But he grew up feeling pressure and expectation from his parents and extended family to live a certain way, to act a certain way, to be a certain way. And all of this took a toll on him over time.

He went to college and studied the thing that his parents wanted him to study in order for him to achieve and be successful and stable and safe in the world, like most parents would want their kids to do. It's a parent's number one concern to constantly be worried about their kid's future. So making sure that they have a stable source of income and a sense of identity is a pretty reasonable expectation, but that certainly comes along with a lot of work.

Finally, at a certain point, he has the opportunity to break free, to go out into the world as a stable individual, to live on his own, to work and live on his own terms. In this person's mind, this is a type of freedom: to work according to his own expectations for himself, to pay his own bills, to buy what he wants when he wants, eat what he wants when he wants, and spend time with whoever he wants when he wants.

I'm not describing the prodigal son. This is a free person making the choices that he wants, sometimes good, sometimes bad, like all of us. But in reality, like many people, the free choices that he has made leave little priority in his life for the word of God, participation in the life of the church, and engagement in his faith-based community. Those are not absent or rejected, but experientially nonexistent, like so many people that we all know.

This person is excited about the opportunity that his lifestyle is going to give him. But what does he find over time? Like almost everybody that we know, this path in his life has eventually led him to be utterly and completely stressed out all the time. He does not know what his future will bring him. He is constantly feeling isolated or lonely or unsure if he's making the right choices. He's having trouble connecting with other people in his life. And all of this is making this great young person pretty unhappy in the midst of all of his freedom.

Again, this isn't the prodigal son. He hasn't gone and lived a horrible life. He just went and got busy in the world doing the things that people do in the world. Why? Why did this freedom that he had worked towards become so detrimental to his well-being? Wouldn't we want him to be happy and confident and comfortable?

St. Paul today, echoing the words of Jesus Christ, gives us great insight into why this is the case. As he made his free choices over time, he didn't realize it, but he traded one master, his Father in heaven, for a thousand other masters that do not care about him. He wakes up in the morning, and his phone tells him when to wake up and what he should be looking at and thinking about before he gets out of bed. He looks at his bills and his credit cards, and that tells him how much he needs to be working in order to pay off his debt, to be considered stable and secure in life and society as his parents and other people expect him to be. He looks at social media and the people around him to have a sense of what's expected of him in terms of what he wears and where he goes and how he spends his time.

He has a thousand indifferent masters, and not one of them can give him what he needs. So naturally, he is unsatisfied, stressed out, and unhappy. Because the freedom that he has is the freedom that turned him into a slave of the world.

But the passage that we read today in the Gospel of Luke is a passage that teaches us about true freedom. We hear about Christ's own reading of the Holy Scriptures. He opens it up, and He reads from the prophets. And He says, “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me because he anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, and to let the oppressed go free, and to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor.” And He rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. And all of the eyes of the temple were fixed upon Him.

Christ came preaching freedom to the captives, to give sight to the blind, to comfort the oppressed. And in this world, you do not have to be a slave in the true sense of the word to be held captive. Many of us are captives in this life because we have chosen a lifestyle that leaves us beholden to the expectations of the world. But Christ says, “I free you from that. I release you from this. You do not need to be a slave. Accept me as your master, and this will liberate you because I can give you life. They can't give back. They can only take. I can give.”

Paul today, in our passage from Galatians, comforts while at the same time admonishing that church, letting them know that they have been freed from the captivity of sin, from the life that would lead them into perdition and suffering. But despite that fact, they want to turn back to their old masters. They want to knowingly and willingly enslave themselves to the world that causes them suffering. But he tells them to be bold, to be brave, and put their faith in their God, in Jesus Christ, and it is by that faith that they will remain saved. Reading forward a little bit, in chapter five of Galatians, he tells them, “For freedom Christ has set us free. Stand firm, therefore, and do not submit yourselves to the yoke of slavery.”

This morning, I was at the airport flying back from Los Angeles, and I saw a man wearing a shirt that said “Freedom,” with an American flag and an eagle, and underneath it said “Galatians 5:1.” I was preparing for my message today, and I had to ask myself, “What does he think freedom is? What do we think freedom is?”

The subtext of that T-shirt tells me that some of us think freedom is the right to not have anything put upon us, any expectation or rule that would govern our lives; that true liberty is the fact that we should plot the course of our own destiny. But within Christianity, true freedom is found in submission: submission to the will of God, submission to the righteousness of our Lord.

Above all else, I want you to imagine a kite. A kite soars through the air upon the winds because it's guided. It's guided to the place where it's going to be lifted up. If you were to cut that string that holds that kite back, would it fly higher or would it fall to the ground? It would fall. Without anything to hold it where it belongs, without anything to keep it where it needs to be, it would fail. It would not be what it was intended to be.

And so this is life in the church. This is the law of love that is inscribed on our hearts. This is what liberty is for each and every one of us: to willingly be bound by the divine ordinance of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

So today, let us be a part of that salvific message, that liberating message, by sharing it with the world and letting other people know that we all have the hope of freedom in our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. That is found when we place upon our necks the light yoke that is life in the church, when we carry that cross in our life as we are meant to be cross-bearers.

For His glory, in the name of the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
