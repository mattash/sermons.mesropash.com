I want you to join me as we consider how we get to this point in the Gospel of St. Matthew, where we read about this story known as the Transfiguration, which I just reread for most of you who are present here today. How did we get to this point where the disciples are standing with Jesus Christ on a mountain known as Tabor — Peter, James, and John — and they see this miraculous event. Jesus is shown bright as the sun, white as the snow. They're overwhelmed with awe and fear. They bow down before him. They hear God's voice: "This is my Son with whom I am well pleased. Listen to him." What is happening at this point in time? Who are these people that are witnessing this? I want to put you in the shoes of one of the people that was brought to this point to understand what is being conceived in this moment in time and how that matters to us.

You are Peter. You're living a simple life. I know none of us feel like we're living simple lives. I don't feel like I do, and I know most of you, and I don't think that you would think that you're living simple lives either. But in the grand scheme of things, we wake up and we do our job and we try our best and we try to support our families, and then we go to sleep again and we get through it day by day by day. In this way, we can identify with the Peter that Jesus finds as he's working as a fisherman on the sea and calls him despite having zero qualifications or any background necessary to become the first and primary disciple of the Savior of the world. And he calls him. And if you put yourself in his position, you're confused. Why should I follow this guy? Who is he? And then he does a miracle. He catches all these fish out of nowhere.
